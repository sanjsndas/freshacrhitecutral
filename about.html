About the Journal

A quiet corner of the internet for people who care about how buildings and interiors shape daily life.

Our Mission

Fresh Architectural Thinking was founded in 2018 as an antidote to the fast, shallow content that dominates design media. We believe that architecture and interior design are not just about aesthetics — they are about how we live, how we feel, and how we relate to the world around us. Our goal is to publish writing that is thoughtful, practical, and deeply researched, without ever being dry.

We are independent. We do not accept sponsored posts, and we do not chase trends. Every article is written by our small editorial team, and we take the time to verify facts, interview practitioners, and consider the long view. Whether you are a homeowner planning a renovation, a student of architecture, or simply someone who loves thoughtful spaces, we hope you find something useful here.

What We Cover

Our articles span the full spectrum of the built environment — from the scale of a city block to the curve of a door handle. We focus on:

  • Interior Design — colour, texture, furniture, and the psychology of rooms.
  • Architecture — residential and public buildings, facades, and the design process.
  • Materials — honest, sustainable, and beautiful materials for every surface.
  • Space Planning — floor plans, circulation, light, and how to make a space work.
  • Lighting — natural and artificial light, layered schemes, and the science of seeing.
  • Home Design — kitchens, bathrooms, bedrooms, and the details that matter daily.

We publish long-form essays, practical guides, interviews, and occasional case studies. New articles appear every week, and our Sunday newsletter, Design Letter, delivers one thoughtful piece directly to your inbox.

Editorial Approach

We write for readers who want to understand, not just consume. That means we avoid jargon where plain language works, and we explain the “why” behind every recommendation. Our articles are structured to be read in one sitting, but we also include enough depth that you can return to them as a reference.

We are not afraid to be critical. If a trend is shallow, we will say so. If a material is overrated, we will explain why. But we also celebrate what is good: the quiet intelligence of a well-planned home, the craft of a beautifully made window, the pleasure of a room that simply feels right.

“We believe good design is not about trends. It is about paying attention — to light, to material, to the way a door handle feels in your hand. This journal is our attempt to slow down and look closely.”
— Elena Marchetti, Editor

The People Behind the Journal

Elena Marchetti

Editor & Founder

Elena trained as an architect in Milan and worked in residential practice for a decade before turning to writing. She founded Fresh Architectural Thinking to create a space for slower, more thoughtful design journalism. She writes about architecture, space planning, and the intersection of design and daily life.

Clara Nyström

Senior Editor, Interiors

Clara is an interior designer and writer based in Stockholm. She has a particular interest in colour, textiles, and the ways small domestic details — a well-placed lamp, a smooth handle — can transform a home. She leads our interior design coverage and edits the Home Design section.

Jonas Weber

Contributing Editor, Materials & Architecture

Jonas is a Berlin-based architect and researcher. He writes about material innovation, sustainable construction, and the poetics of building. His work often explores the tension between craft and industry, and he is a passionate advocate for honest, local materials.

Mira Patel

Contributing Editor, Lighting & Wellbeing

Mira is a lighting designer and environmental psychologist. She writes about how light affects our mood, sleep, and productivity, and offers practical advice for improving the lighting in any home. She is based in London and lectures on lighting design at a leading art school.

Transparency & Funding

Fresh Architectural Thinking is reader-supported. We earn revenue through optional paid subscriptions to our newsletter, and occasionally through affiliate links to books or products we genuinely recommend. Affiliate links are always clearly disclosed at the top of the relevant article. We do not accept sponsored content, and we do not allow advertisers to influence our editorial decisions.

All articles are written by our editorial team. If we ever publish a guest contribution, it will be clearly marked, and we will only work with writers whose values align with our own.

Contact & Corrections

We welcome thoughtful correspondence, corrections, and pitches. If you spot an error, please let us know — we are committed to accuracy and will correct mistakes promptly.
